### Choosing a Database

Since we use AWS, the candidates were roughly [RDS](https://aws.amazon.com/jp/rds/), [DynamoDB](https://aws.amazon.com/jp/dynamodb/), and [DocumentDB](https://aws.amazon.com/jp/documentdb/). I ruled out DocumentDB because it's expensive. I also added [MongoDB](https://www.mongodb.com/) to the list, since it recently got a serverless offering and the price is cheap at 0.3 USD per million reads per day (and at our current scale, the free tier is more than enough).

The data I have in mind is limited to things like user information and content information. It's also a young service, so the usage patterns haven't fully settled, and breaking changes may come later. With that in mind, here are the pros and cons of each database.

#### RDS

- △: [Flexibility] An RDBMS. As long as you normalize your data, you can pull it out flexibly with queries. Schema changes are a pain, though.
- △: [Scalability] To handle a sudden jump in users, you have to think about things like auto-scaling settings.
- ×: [Cost] Has a free tier. Since it's an always-on instance, you're billed even when it isn't being used.

#### DynamoDB

- ×: [Flexibility] A KVS. Because the schema is denormalized, a single change may require a Scan. Designing a schema while the usage patterns are still unsettled is also risky.
- ◯: [Scalability] Scales horizontally, so it can quickly handle a sudden jump in users.
- ◯: [Cost] Has a free tier. Billing is based on the number of reads and writes, so it's relatively cheap.

#### MongoDB

- △: [Flexibility] A document-oriented DB. It supports joins like an RDB, so you can lay out data with normalization in mind. Schema changes are easy too. However, moving from the free tier to the serverless version may currently involve downtime? (needs checking)
- ◯: [Scalability] Scales horizontally, so it can quickly handle a sudden jump in users.
- ◯: [Cost] Has a free tier. Billing is based on the number of reads and writes, so it's relatively cheap.

Putting all of this together, I'm currently leaning toward MongoDB. If I end up needing simple but high-volume I/O, like logs, I might consider DynamoDB. If you have other recommendations, please let me know in the comments.
